    Short answer

    Drop your SVG above, choose ICO and download one .ico with the logo at 16, 32 and 48 pixels, transparency kept. It runs in your browser, so nothing is uploaded. You also get a 256 px size when the SVG is at least 256 px wide, or has no size of its own (it is then drawn 1024 px wide). A non-square logo is centred on a transparent square.

    Updated 25 November 2026

    Why start from an SVG?

    An SVG is drawn fresh at every size, so each icon size starts from a crisp image instead of a scaled-down photo. Logos made in a design tool are often already SVG, with a transparent background, which is exactly what an icon needs.

    SVG and ICO compared

    CompressionVector: shapes, not pixelsSeveral small PNG icons in one file
    TransparencyYesYes

    Opens inBrowsers and design tools; many apps and forms can'tWindows, and browsers as a favicon

    Do I still need an .ico if browsers accept SVG favicons?

    Current browsers accept an SVG favicon through a <link rel="icon"> tag, but a favicon.ico at your site's root is still what many browsers, bookmarks and tools look for first. Many sites provide both.

    My logo looks too thin at 16 px

    Fine lines and small text vanish at icon sizes. A simplified version of the logo, such as just the symbol or the first letter, with thicker strokes, reads far better in a browser tab.

    Questions

    Does SVG to ICO keep transparency?

    Yes. Areas the SVG leaves empty stay transparent in every size of the icon.

    Which sizes are in the file?

    16, 32 and 48 px, plus 256 px when the SVG is at least that wide or has no size of its own. Each is stored as a PNG inside the .ico.

    Can I get a PNG favicon instead?

    Yes, see SVG to PNG, then set the size with Maximum width.
