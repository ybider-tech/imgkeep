    How do I convert an image without uploading it?

    Use a converter that does the work in your browser, like this one. Your files are read by the page on your own device and converted there, with the same code as the Imgkeep extension. Nothing is sent to a server, so there's nothing to delete afterwards and no account to create.

    You can check it: load the page, turn off your Wi-Fi and convert a file. It still works. Or watch your browser's Network tab while converting: no request carries your image.

    Can I paste the result instead of downloading it?

    Yes. Every result has Copy as PNG: it puts the image on your clipboard as a real PNG, transparency kept, ready to paste into Canva, Figma, Google Slides or a chat. See getting an image into Canva, Figma or Google Slides.

    What are WebP and AVIF, and why won't my apps open them?

    Many sites now send images as WebP or AVIF: smaller files that browsers show fine, but that older apps, upload forms and some design tools refuse. Converting to PNG or JPG fixes that. More in why Chrome saves images as WebP and how to save AVIF as JPG or PNG.

    What are the limits?

    • Up to 20 files at once, each up to 50 MB.
    • GIFs are at most 800 px wide and 600 frames; animation is kept in Chrome and Edge.
    • ICO files hold 16, 32 and 48 px, plus 256 px when the image is at least that big.
    • Safari can't make WebP files, so WebP output isn't offered there.
    • HEIC photos (from iPhones) can't be opened in Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
    • Very large images are slower on phones.

    Is there a faster way for images on web pages?

    Yes: the Imgkeep extension for Chrome and Edge does the same in one right-click, before you even have a file: right-click any image, choose Imgkeep: Save image as, then the format. It also finds photos that sites like Instagram and Pinterest hide under another layer.
