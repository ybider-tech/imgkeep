    How local conversion works

    Your images are converted by this page, on your device, with the same code as the Imgkeep extension. Nothing is uploaded. The site counts page views, and which formats were converted with how many files (for example "webp → jpg, 3 files"); it never sends image contents, file names or file sizes.

    • Load this page and add a photo, then turn off your Wi-Fi: converting still works. (In Chrome, Edge and Firefox the HEIC decoder loads with your first photo; Safari doesn't need it.)
    • Open your browser's developer tools, choose the Network tab and convert a file: no request carries your image.
    • The code is public: site/convert on GitHub, with a test that checks converting makes no network request and works offline.

    Short answer

    Drop your HEIC photos above, choose PDF and download one PDF for each photo, exactly the photo's size and the right way up. In Chrome, Edge and Firefox the page first loads a HEIC decoder (about 0.5 MB); Safari opens HEIC itself. Everything happens in your browser, so nothing is uploaded, which matters for IDs and receipts.

    Updated 25 November 2026

    Why turn an iPhone photo into a PDF?

    Forms and offices often want documents as PDF, and many can't open HEIC at all. Photographing a receipt or an ID with your iPhone gives a HEIC file; this turns it into a PDF that opens anywhere.

    Is it private enough for an ID or a bank document?

    The photo never leaves your device: the page reads and converts it in your browser, and it keeps working offline once the decoder has loaded. Nothing is sent to a server. Only the main photo is converted, not Live Photo video or depth data.

    How do I keep the PDF small?

    iPhone photos are large, often 4000 pixels wide. Set a Maximum width of about 2000 in Advanced options: documents stay readable and the PDF is far smaller. To print, choose "Fit to page" in the print dialog.

    Questions

    Can I combine several photos into one PDF?

    Not here: each photo becomes its own PDF. A PDF reader or editor that merges files can combine them.

    Does it work on iPhone?

    Yes, in Safari on iPhone and iPad, where HEIC opens without the extra decoder.

    What about a JPG instead?

    For a photo to share rather than a document, HEIC to JPG gives a smaller file every app opens.
